# Interview Presentation Refresh Design

Date: 2026-07-25

## Goal

Create a new 10-minute Chinese interview presentation for an AI Algorithm
Engineer. The deck should present Zhentai Zhang as an experienced,
delivery-oriented engineer rather than a recent graduate giving an academic
defense.

The former presentation `zzt-250303.pptx` is a content reference only. Its
visual template does not constrain the new deck.

## Communication Job

By the end of the presentation, interviewers should understand that Zhentai can
connect computer-vision research, multimodal knowledge engineering, and
production Agent applications to deliver complete AI systems.

## Narrative Approach

Use a project-case-led narrative:

1. Establish the current professional positioning.
2. Summarize the career path and technical scope.
3. Demonstrate current delivery capability through two anonymized work cases.
4. Support the engineering story with iFLYTEK computer-vision and multimodal
   experience.
5. Show the research foundation through GCLR and Glo-MMF.
6. Close with transferable value for an AI algorithm team.

Do not mention agriculture experience. Do not claim TensorFlow, model
lightweighting, inference acceleration, or Jetson deployment experience.

## Visual Direction

- Build the deck from scratch in 16:9.
- Use a mature corporate visual system: deep navy, warm gray-white, and a
  restrained teal accent.
- Use Chinese as the primary language, with English only for established
  technical terms.
- Favor strong typography, disciplined whitespace, flat composition, and
  evidence-led layouts.
- Avoid academic-defense styling, school-brand decoration, decorative
  gradients, dense card dashboards, and gratuitous diagrams.
- Use the old headshot only if it remains visually appropriate; otherwise leave
  a clearly replaceable portrait slot.
- Render undisclosed metrics as conspicuous but professional editable
  placeholders such as `[待补：知识库规模]`.

## Evidence and Privacy

- Expand one or two Guangdong Xingwen work cases anonymously.
- Do not expose client, patient, product, or confidential internal names.
- Do not invent outcomes or quantitative claims.
- Put citations for publications and externally sourced claims in speaker notes.
- Reuse user-owned figures and screenshots without treating them as external
  evidence.
